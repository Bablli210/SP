Rule1

The object of the game

Build a website that proves the studio's method within ten seconds. The portfolio is the proof.

Serious Play already has its thesis: “We create brands rooted by serious research and driven by playful imagination.” The new site should behave like that sentence.

What we heard

  • A concept website that revolves around the portfolio.
  • Inspired by Studio Gray Lab and a set of Instagram references you chose.
  • Room to be experimental, with the craft level of a fashion or luxury studio.
  • The current site is minimal. The next one should carry the studio's personality.

Win conditions

  • A new visitor can say what Serious Play does after one screen.
  • Every case study shows the research behind the result, as well as the result.
  • The site feels like a Serious Play project, with one interaction people remember and share.
  • Browsing the work stays fast and clear, on a phone as much as on a desktop.
  • The team can add a new project without calling a developer.

Rule2

The players

Three kinds of visitor, each arriving with one question. The site answers all three without making anyone dig.

Future clients

Can they do this for my brand? Show me work like mine, and show me how you think.
  • Work filtered by sector and service
  • Case studies that lead with the finding
  • Clear services and one obvious way to get in touch

Future team

Is this a place where I would do my best work?
  • The method, told honestly
  • The people and the studio space
  • Open roles and how to apply

Press, juries and partners

What's the story here, and who made it?
  • Studio facts in one paragraph
  • Full credits on every project
  • A press kit with logos and portraits

Words we play with

RigorousEvery claim on the site is backed by a project.
CuriousReward people who poke around.
TactileThings respond to the hand: weight, friction, light.
WittyOne smile per page. Never at the expense of clarity.
PreciseTight grids, exact type, nothing accidental.
GenerousShow the process, share the thinking.

Words we avoid:QuirkyZanyCorporateMinimal for its own sake

Rule4

The pieces

Six art direction principles. They hold in every direction, so whichever one you choose, the brand stays the same.

play

Two layers, always

Every page has a serious layer (the grid, the facts, the research) and a play layer (one interaction, one surprise).

DoPair each finding with the finished work.Don'tAdd play that has no idea behind it.
AaA rigorous face for information. A characterful face for the voice.

Type does the talking

Two families, big sizes, few weights. Headlines carry the personality so the layout can stay calm.

DoSet headlines large enough to feel like objects.Don'tMix more than two families on a page.

The work carries the colour

The interface stays neutral so the portfolio can be as loud as each client needs.

DoLet one project take over the screen.Don'tBrand the frame louder than the work.

A strict grid you can break

Twelve columns and an 8px rhythm. A play moment may break the grid, once per screen.

DoBreak the grid on purpose, with one element.Don'tLet everything float.

Motion with weight

Things ease, settle and respond to the hand. Every effect has a calm version for people who turn motion off.

DoAnimate in answer to what the visitor does.Don'tFade everything in on scroll.
What do you do?We research brands, then play with them until they work.

Talk like a smart friend

Plain words, short sentences, specific claims. Wit is welcome once the point is clear.

DoWrite “We found 72% of buyers were gifting.”Don'tWrite “We craft meaningful experiences.”

Our recommendation

Lead with Lights Off. Keep the depth of Field Notes. Hide a Playground inside.

  1. Home and Work use Lights Off. The flashlight and the light switch give the luxury first impression your references point to, and the dial sits in the middle, where the studio's name does.
  2. Case studies use the Field Notes dossier. Each one opens with its finding, so the research is always on show.
  3. The Playground becomes the Lab and the 404 page. A place for experiments, and a reward for the curious.

Prefer one pure direction? Each prototype stands on its own, and the plan works for any of them.

The case study, in order

  1. Title and finding. The one insight that unlocked the project.
  2. The brief. What the client asked for, in two sentences.
  3. The research. Interviews, audits, sketches, rejected routes.
  4. The idea. How the finding became a brand.
  5. The work. Full-bleed images and video.
  6. The result. Numbers or quotes, when the client allows.
  7. Credits and next study.

How we'd build it

Front endAstro, with GSAP and Lenis for motion and Matter.js only where physics is needed
ContentSanity CMS, so the team adds projects, images and credits themselves
HostingVercel, with preview links for every change
AlternativeFramer or Webflow for a faster, designer-edited build, with simpler interactions

What we need from you

  • 8–12 projects, each with one finding, research material, 8–15 images and credits
  • Studio and team photography, or a shoot we plan together
  • Services, client list and any press
  • Logo files, brand fonts and social handles
  • One person who signs off each gate
Quality bar
  • Every interaction has a calm version for reduced motion, and a keyboard way through.
  • Accessible to WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast, focus, alt text and captions.
  • Fast on a mid-range phone: a Lighthouse performance score of 90 or more on key pages.
  • Search basics built in: titles, descriptions, social cards and structured data for projects.

Rule7

Your move

Three decisions and eight questions. Answer them and design starts the following week.

A

Pick a direction

One of the three, or the recommended mix. Set the dial at the top of this page to show us where you lean.

B

Choose the launch work

8–12 projects that best show the range you want to be hired for.

C

Approve the plan

The ten-week timeline, the sitemap and who signs off each gate.

Questions for you

  1. Which reference matters most, and why?Tell us what you love in it: type, colour, motion, layout or attitude.
  2. Who is the client you most want to win next?Sector, size and region. The site should speak to them first.
  3. Which projects must be on the site at launch?And do you still have their research material: interviews, sketches, rejected routes?
  4. Does the site need a second language?A right-to-left language such as Arabic changes type and layout choices, so we plan for it from the start.
  5. Where is the studio based, and should the site say so?City, local time and address can all become part of the design.
  6. Who updates the site after launch, and how often?This decides the CMS and how much we automate.
  7. Do you have photography and video of the team and the work?If not, we plan a shoot during the design phase.
  8. Is there a launch date or event we're aiming for?And a budget range for the build, so we can size the interactions to match.
